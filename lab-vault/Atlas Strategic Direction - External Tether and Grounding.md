## Strategic premise — the tether-driven phase

Atlas / Relational Labs has developed enough internal machinery that its next
major risk is self-sealing: producing increasingly coherent internal structures
without forcing those structures into contact with external scientific problems
capable of falsifying them.

The next phase is tether-driven. A strong external anchor is:

1. **External** — the question exists independently of Atlas.
2. **Adversarial** — the comparison can expose redundancy, frame dependence,
   inadequacy, or failure.
3. **Numbered** — success, null, and failure are expressed through declared
   measurements.
4. **Literature-hostile** — the work meets the strongest relevant prior art and
   conventional alternatives rather than choosing an easy gap.
5. **Can't-lose** — the experiment remains scientifically useful under either a
   positive or null outcome.

“Can't-lose” does **not** mean Atlas cannot be falsified. A null must be able to
kill or constrain an Atlas interpretation. A positive must be independently
measurable and reproducible. The experiment remains valuable when Atlas loses.

## Two parallel tracks

Grounding does not block external work, and external work does not excuse an
ungrounded object.

### Track A — Ground the object

Priority:

1. GA2 — Observer / Foliation Bundle Audit
2. GA1 — Invariant Correspondence Audit

Purpose: determine what kind of objects GCS, parity, and signature structures
actually are.

### Track B — Externalize the machine

Begin EA1 immediately using `E_resolved`, `E_compressed`, and `Delta_E`, without
requiring GCS extraction as the primary result. Develop EA3 next.

Purpose: determine whether Atlas diagnostics measure useful compression loss or
model inadequacy against external baselines.

The tracks inform one another; neither blocks the other.

## External anchors

### EA1 — Controlled Noncommutation Under Gravitational Compression

**Status: WISHED-FOR external-anchor program. Start now. Can't-lose grade: 5/5.**

Compare a fully source-resolved gravitational scene with declared compressed
surrogates. The default serious witness is the registered electric-Weyl tensor:

```text
E_resolved(x)
E_compressed(x)
Delta_E(x) = E_resolved(x) - E_compressed(x)
```

`Delta_E` is not a physical source, force, energy density, or independent field.
It is a registered tensor response between solved/evaluated scenes under a
declared compression intervention. Point identification, frame transport,
reference metric, normalization, and source representation must be declared
before subtraction.

Characterize:

- Frobenius residual amplitude and normalized residual ratio;
- eigenvalue and eigenstructure changes;
- angular and radial coherence;
- spatial morphology;
- source-count dependence;
- arrangement and source-morphology dependence;
- scale dependence; and
- downstream geodesic-deviation or trajectory consequences where appropriate.

Where useful, formalize the experiment-specific noncommutation question as:

```text
C o S  versus  S o C
```

Here `S` means solve/evaluate gravitational structure and `C` means
compress/coarse-grain the source description. These are not presumed universal
operators. Each experiment must declare what its `S` and `C` do.

Public/external framing:

> Atlas provides a controlled finite-source laboratory for measuring
> gravitational information lost, preserved, or reorganized when
> source-resolved descriptions are replaced by compressed surrogates.

Cosmological averaging and backreaction are adjacent literature and motivation.
This near-zone experiment does not claim to solve or directly test cosmological
backreaction. It continues the Relational State Compression and mesoscale
program under stricter external comparison.

Useful outcome classes include:

- residuals rapidly decohere or scale toward zero;
- residuals remain coherent and grow with source complexity;
- only selected tensor/eigenstructure channels survive compression; or
- the result is strongly registration- or frame-dependent.

All four constrain the program.

### EA2 — Literature Boundary Audit: Quasilocal Energy / Curvature Attribution

**Status: WISHED-FOR literature audit, not an immediate build. Immediate-anchor
grade: 2/5. Literature-awareness importance: 5/5.**

`Delta_i E` is **not** a gravitational energy density. It measures a registered
tidal/free-curvature response between solved scenes under a declared
intervention.

Before any build, perform a read-hard audit of ADM and Bondi concepts where
relevant, Brown–York, Hawking-type quasilocal constructions, Wang–Yau,
gravitational-energy localization, and Weyl-based curvature-attribution
literature.

Ask:

1. Are we asking the same question?
2. Is the apparent gap already occupied?
3. Is the Atlas construction complementary?
4. Is this adjacency rather than a research lane?

**Know the boundary before entering.**

### EA3 — Reduced-Order Model Validity and Adaptive-Resolution Failure Prediction

**Status: WISHED-FOR external-anchor program. Can't-lose grade: 5/5.**

External question: can Atlas diagnostics identify where a coarse or reduced
gravitational representation becomes inadequate before conventional bulk-error
measures do?

Representative registered residual:

```text
R_E(x) = ||E_resolved - E_reduced||_F
         / (||E_resolved||_F + epsilon)
```

This connects Atlas to numerical relativity, multiscale simulation,
surrogate/reduced-order modeling, adaptive refinement, boundary inheritance,
and computational physics. Compare against conventional task-matched baselines
and downstream targets such as trajectory error, geodesic-deviation error,
constraint error, boundary-transfer error, reconstruction error, waveform
discrepancy where physically appropriate, and computational cost at matched
accuracy.

Critical falsification question:

> Does Atlas predict anything useful that conventional error/refinement
> diagnostics do not?

If no, kill or demote the computational-utility branch. If yes, this becomes a
major external application independent of stronger identity interpretations.

## Grounding audits

### GA1 — Invariant Correspondence Audit

**Status: WISHED-FOR grounding audit.**

Test existing certified parity/signature structures against appropriate
invariant curvature structure, potentially including scalar curvature
invariants, Weyl invariants, algebraic-speciality measures, and Petrov
structure/type where technically meaningful.

Question: do Atlas loci systematically correspond to invariant curvature
structures?

- **Strong correspondence:** Atlas may extract a recognizable manifestation of
  invariant curvature organization.
- **Partial correspondence:** Atlas may be an observer/slice-dependent
  projection of deeper invariant structure.
- **Little or no correspondence:** the object may belong primarily to the
  registered observer/slice/context diagnostic layer.

The third outcome is not automatically failure. The audit classifies the kind
of object present. Correlation with a curvature invariant does not by itself
anchor a GCS “in the absolute.”

### GA2 — Observer / Foliation Bundle Audit

**Status: WISHED-FOR grounding audit; highest-priority grounding lane.**

The existing Brill–Lindquist/time-symmetric lane has `K_ij = 0`, a special
initial-data slice. Test Atlas objects across a declared family of slicings,
Eulerian normals `n^mu`, electric/magnetic Weyl decompositions `E_ij`/`B_ij`,
and point/frame registration choices where appropriate.

Ask which structures persist, move smoothly, transform predictably, disappear,
or reveal themselves as artifacts of the chosen observer or slice. Do not force
the result into a binary invariant/non-invariant classification. An
**observer-response family** for a relational structure may itself become an
Atlas evidence object.

## Doctrine corrections and provenance

### DC1 — Multi-source GR accuracy

General relativity has no closed-form generic two-body solution analogous to the
isolated Kerr solution. Multi-source systems generally require approximation,
special exact constructions, initial-data solutions, perturbation theory, or
numerical relativity. Brill–Lindquist provides exact time-symmetric,
constraint-satisfying multi-black-hole initial data away from punctures; it is
not the complete dynamical N-body spacetime.

Do not replace the incorrect idea that “GR is exact for one and two bodies” with
the equally overbroad claim that everything with `N >= 2` is approximate. Name
the construction, equations solved, slice, exclusions, and claim ceiling.

### DC2 — Static/time-symmetric provenance

Use this qualifier for static/time-symmetric findings unless a later result
earns a broader statement:

> Established/Reproduced in the declared time-symmetric initial-data lane.
> Persistence under foliation change and dynamical evolution remains untested
> unless separately stated.

This is provenance, not apologetic hedging.

The electric-Weyl tensor `E_ij` remains the default serious GR-facing witness.
GCS/parity loci are derived diagnostic objects, never physical walls,
membranes, sources, or force cutoffs. Renders remain instruments and witnesses,
not proof.

## Governance

### GV1 — Found / Wished-For Discipline

Every strategic object should be legible as **FOUND** or **WISHED-FOR** where
appropriate. Interpretation-rich proposals default to WISHED-FOR until they
clear the current canonical claim ladder. Hypotheses, attractive readings,
rendered morphology, implemented diagnostics, reproduced results, and
stabilized findings are not one category.

This sheet does not redefine, insert, remove, or reorder claim rungs. Claim
status remains governed by current canonical adjudication and object-specific
receipts.

### GV2 — Wing Asymmetry

- **Claude wing:** reach, territory finding, synthesis, conceptual connection,
  and visual/conceptual exploration.
- **GPT wing:** adjudication, falsification pressure, measurement audit,
  doctrine integration, and claim promotion.
- **Codex:** implementation, repo/vault execution, deterministic artifact
  production, preservation, and logging.
- **Grok:** sealed innovation/speculation wing under existing doctrine.

Do not average these pressures into bland consensus. Cross-wing disagreement is
an experimental asset.

### GV3 — Registered Comparison Before Interpretation

Resolved/ablated/compressed comparisons require declared point identification,
frame transport, normalization, approximation class, and intervention. Tensor
subtraction occurs only after both objects occupy a declared common comparison
bundle.

### GV4 — Every External Anchor Requires a Non-Atlas Baseline

Atlas must not compare only Atlas to Atlas. Every external-tether experiment
includes at least one conventional, task-appropriate baseline.

- Compression: barycenter/monopole surrogate, multipole compression,
  smoothing/coarse-graining, or another conventional reduced representation.
- Adaptive refinement: truncation-error criteria or conventional
  curvature/error indicators.
- Persistence: standard invariant curvature measures.
- Morphology: conventional moments or established geometric descriptors.
- Dynamics: conventional trajectories, geodesic deviation, and established
  invariant/evolution diagnostics.

The governing question is **“Does Atlas add information?”**, not merely “Does
Atlas produce structure?”

### GV5 — Promote Predictive Compression Before Interpretive Ontology

Near-term priority favors externally checkable statements:

- this tensor diagnostic predicted where a coarse model failed;
- this residual scaled with source complexity according to a measured relation;
- this feature survived a declared family of lawful slicings; or
- this eigenstructure change predicted a measurable downstream trajectory
  difference.

Only after such statements are earned should stronger identity interpretation
be promoted. Finite-source relational identity remains a live downstream
hypothesis supported by measurable persistence, not internal elegance alone.

## Closing direction

The next phase of Relational Labs is not primarily about producing more internal
Atlas structure. It is about placing Atlas diagnostics against external
problems, conventional baselines, controlled nulls, and hostile comparisons
capable of proving the machinery unnecessary, frame-bound, redundant, or wrong.

A strong Atlas result must increasingly survive questions that existed before
Atlas.

**The machine is built enough to be challenged. Now point it at things that can
fight back.**
