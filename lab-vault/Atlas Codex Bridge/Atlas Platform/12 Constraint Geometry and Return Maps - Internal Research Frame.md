## Governing discipline

These lanes ask whether Atlas reductions omit important structure. The burden is
to define ambient variables, constraints, projections, norms or bilinear forms,
registrations, loops, comparison rules, and null expectations before computing.
Elegant geometry words are not evidence.

Three fences apply throughout:

1. A local derivative is not automatically a tangent space.
2. An approximation family is not automatically a submanifold of the nonlinear
   GR solution set, and a residual norm is not automatically intrinsic distance.
3. A loop nonclosure is not automatically holonomy, memory, genealogy,
   emergence, or new physics.

## CG-1 — Family S constraint response

**Status:** RESERVED CERTIFICATION LANE / ZERO PROMOTION.

Let the declared constraint map be `F(x) = 0`, where `x` names a fully specified
field representation and gauge/constraint setting for Family S. A screened
operator may be built from a local derivative or Jacobian such as `DF_x`, with
the screening rule, discretization, boundary conditions, and regularization
declared.

The screened operator is **not** to be called “the tangent space.” Legitimate
tangent variations at a certified solution must satisfy the linearized
constraint equation, for example

`DF_x[delta x] = 0`

with any gauge quotient, boundary conditions, and function-space assumptions
made explicit. Kernel approximations, truncated singular spaces, regularized
responses, and screened directions must retain those narrower names.

Finite Atlas ablations are finite displacements between declared scenes. They
are not infinitesimal tangent vectors merely because their amplitude is small or
because a Jacobian is later evaluated.

### Reserved epsilon test

A later certification packet may define an epsilon family `x(epsilon)` and test:

- constraint residual scaling with `epsilon`;
- agreement between finite differences and `DF_x[delta x]`;
- gauge and boundary-condition sensitivity;
- basis and resolution stability;
- the range in which a linearized description is empirically supported.

No epsilon test is executed or pre-certified here.

## CG-2 — Approximation spaces and orthogonal residuals

**Status:** RESERVED CERTIFICATION LANE / ZERO PROMOTION.

`V1`, `V2`, and `V3` may denote declared approximation spaces in an **ambient conformal-field representation space**. Their basis, coordinates, regularity,
boundary behavior, gauge treatment, and inclusion relations must be stated.
They are not, by default, submanifolds of the nonlinear Einstein-constraint
solution manifold.

For a declared projection `P_V` and declared positive norm or inner product, a
basis-resistant residual may be written schematically as

`F_perp = (I - P_V) F`

or an appropriately typed analogue. Its value measures the component rejected
by that declared approximation/projection. It is **not** an intrinsic distance
to the nonlinear solution manifold.

Basis resistance must be tested under admissible basis changes within the same
declared space. It does not mean independence from the choice of space,
projection, weighting, gauge, grid, or representation.

The DeWitt supermetric is indefinite. Without additional structure it does not
define a distance, so “nearest,” “orthogonal distance,” and metric projection
language is reserved. Any later use must state exactly what positive structure
or alternative variational principle replaces the missing distance concept.

## TR-1 — Temporal return-map nonclosure

**Status:** RESERVED CERTIFICATION LANE / ZERO PROMOTION. Design now; execute
only after the temporal harness is implemented and certified.

Consider a declared parameter loop

`lambda_0 -> lambda_1 -> ... -> lambda_n = lambda_0`.

Early-stage terms are **temporal return map**, **return residual**, and
**nonclosure**. “Holonomy” is reserved until all of the following are certified:

- a base space;
- a fiber/state space over each base point;
- a connection or transport rule;
- the loop and its admissibility;
- the comparison/identification rule at return;
- gauge, observer, registration, and discretization controls.

### Hierarchical closure battery

A return test must compare, separately and cumulatively:

1. control parameter and source-roster return;
2. `gamma_ij` and `K_ij` return;
3. matter fields and momenta, where present;
4. lapse, shift, gauge-driver, boundary, and refinement state;
5. registration/frame/tetrad return;
6. `E_ij`, `B_ij`, `weyl_complex_ij`, constraints, and invariant-curvature
   witnesses;
7. Atlas diagnostics, track fields, attribution ledgers, and event declarations;
8. compressed/reduced-state return and its declared omitted-state residual.

lambda return does not imply GR-state return. A loop may close in a control
parameter while the evolved fields, gauge, matter, momenta, refinement state, or
checkpoint history do not.

### First interpretation of nonclosure

The first supported interpretation is conservative:

> The declared reduced description omitted dynamically stored state.

Only after full-state, numerical, gauge, observer, registration, extraction,
and backend explanations are controlled may a narrower physical interpretation
be proposed. Nonclosure alone does not establish memory, genealogy, emergence,
hysteresis, holonomy, or new physics.

### Far question

This question guides the lane. It is not an answer and not a doctrine claim.
