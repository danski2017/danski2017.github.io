## Current Synthesis

The public paper line begins with source/context readability and matures into a stronger declaration-and-ledger discipline. GCS I defines the parity horizon. GCS II turns pairwise source readability into networks. GCS III asks whether those networks occupy a real mesoscale diagnostic layer between local evaluation and coarse averaging. GCS IV shows that such structures can be operationally extracted under a declared scalar branch. GCS V expands the question into nested scene declaration and child-parent handoff. The Information Boundaries companion interprets parity as source-attribution uncertainty. GFRO turns the construction into a residual operator and zero-set emission method. The May 2026 breakthrough pair then reframes the program around finite-source curvature identity: budget, jurisdiction, compression, Ricci-side custody, Weyl-electric expression, and an unsolved causal handoff frontier.
