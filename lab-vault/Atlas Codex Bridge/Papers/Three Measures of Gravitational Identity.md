## Core Result

A finite source has at least three separable gravitational identity measures:

| Measure | Atlas reading | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | finite exterior Weyl-electric square content | source mass and radius |
| Jurisdiction | source-context dominance region | surrounding source scene |
| Compression | distance where internal detail can be replaced | declared approximation tolerance |

These measures do not collapse into one another. They answer different questions and should be stored as separate diagnostic channels.

## Key Formula

For a declared spherical source, the exterior Weyl-electric square budget is:

```text
B_E = 8 pi G^2 M^2 / R^3
```

The paper's major scaling insight is that budget per unit mass is proportional to mean density. This makes nuclear-density objects curvature-efficient across radically different sizes.

## Scale Ladder Findings

The paper's budget ladder is a conceptual turning point:

- proton and neutron star budget-per-mass values are comparable because both sit near nuclear density,
- hydrogen atoms fall off a cliff because atomic radius spreads the same proton mass over a much larger effective scale,
- black holes invert the budget relation when `R = 2GM/c^2`, giving `B_E = pi c^6 / (G M)`,
- the budget peaks near the neutron star / black hole boundary inside the declared spherical-source model.
