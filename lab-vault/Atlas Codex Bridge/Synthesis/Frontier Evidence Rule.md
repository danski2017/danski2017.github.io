# Frontier Evidence Rule

## Status

Doctrine patch / lab steering rule / no new science claim.

## Purpose

Atlas has historically used strong conservative claim discipline to avoid overreach. That discipline remains active. However, the lab now recognizes a second failure mode: over-conservative dismissal of coherent frontier signals because they appear unfamiliar, nonstandard, or in tension with inherited GR bookkeeping conventions.

The purpose of this rule is to prevent both errors:

1. overclaiming beyond ledgered evidence,
2. ignoring lawful signals because they strain convention.

## North Star

Atlas follows data and logic wherever they lead.

GR remains the governing computational law by default. Atlas does not modify the Einstein field equations, introduce new forces, or treat diagnostics as physical sources unless a result explicitly forces such a question.

However, Atlas does not treat current GR workflows, averaging habits, source-compression conventions, boundary-condition habits, or interpretive dogma as final.

If a declared scene, lawful source/datum map, valid calculation, reproducible ledger, dimensional discipline, and independent checks expose a structure that strains convention, Atlas follows the result.

The required response is not dismissal.
The required response is escalation of rigor.

## Core sentence

GR is respected.
Data is followed.
Logic is binding.
Dogma is not a veto.

## Frontier escalation protocol

When a result appears unfamiliar, nonstandard, or in tension with convention, Atlas must not bury it. Instead:

1. Recheck the scene declaration.
2. Recheck source/datum roles.
3. Recheck units and dimensions.
4. Recheck gauge, slicing, coordinates, and observer/tetrad assumptions.
5. Recheck boundary, cutoff, domain, and parent-context declarations.
6. Test alternative formulations.
7. Compare against known GR identities and standard literature.
8. Seek independent reproduction across wings or methods.
9. Lower the claim ceiling until the result survives.
10. Preserve the signal in the vault even if interpretation remains unsettled.

## Distinction ladder

A frontier result must be classified by what it appears to strain:

1. Contradicts an Atlas habit:
   Revise Atlas habit if ledger survives.

2. Contradicts an averaging or compression convention:
   This is central Atlas territory. Escalate rigor and preserve.

3. Contradicts a common GR workflow:
   Serious frontier. Compare against known identities, approximations, and gauge choices.

4. Contradicts a standard interpretation of GR:
   High burden. Preserve as frontier signal only after independent checks.

5. Appears to contradict the Einstein field equations themselves:
   Extraordinary claim. Highest evidence burden. No promotion without repeated derivation, independent reproduction, and external literature comparison.

## No forbidden discoveries

Atlas does not reject a result merely because it is bold.

Forbidden:

- claiming without evidence,
- hiding dimensional mismatch,
- ignoring gauge dependence,
- treating renders as proof,
- smuggling in new physics,
- confusing diagnostics with sources,
- skipping validation.

Not forbidden:

- new diagnostic grammar,
- new bookkeeping language,
- new curvature-custody interpretation,
- new source/context attribution structures,
- new handoff or compression signals,
- results that make inherited conventions uncomfortable.

No forbidden discoveries.
Only forbidden shortcuts.

## Relation to GR discipline

This rule does not weaken GR governance. It strengthens it by requiring unfamiliar results to be tested harder rather than dismissed faster.

Atlas remains GR-governed unless data and logic force a different question.

## Relation to claim boundaries

This rule does not promote any scratch result. It changes the response to frontier signals:
dismissal is not allowed without analysis;
promotion is not allowed without validation.

The middle state is preserved frontier signal.

## Private-lane formulation

GR governs the field.
Atlas investigates the custody grammar.
If the grammar is new, we do not flinch.

## Public-safe formulation

This work does not challenge general relativity as a local field theory. It challenges the assumption that existing bookkeeping conventions exhaust the relational structure available inside GR-governed scenes.
