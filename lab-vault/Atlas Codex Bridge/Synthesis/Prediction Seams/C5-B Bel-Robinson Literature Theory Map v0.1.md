### Literature-grounded anchors

1. Bel-Robinson / superenergy family

The Bel-Robinson tensor is the classical superenergy tensor for the gravitational field, built quadratically from curvature/Weyl. Senovilla's superenergy review places the Bel-Robinson tensor inside a broader family of superenergy tensors and emphasizes that completely timelike components have energy-density-like mathematical properties, including positivity and dominant-property analogues.

Atlas implication:
Bel-Robinson belongs in the curvature-square witness family. It may be appropriate for curvature-custody bookkeeping, but it is not ordinary stress-energy.

Citation target:
Senovilla, "Super-energy tensors," arXiv:gr-qc/9906087.

2. Electric/magnetic Weyl split

Relative to an observer, gravitational curvature can be decomposed into electric and magnetic Weyl parts. The schematic superenergy density relevant to Atlas is:

```text
E_ij E^ij + B_ij B^ij
```

Atlas implication:
C5-A used only the electric-Weyl branch. Dynamic/radiative C5-B must not omit magnetic-Weyl structure without declaring a near-zone/static approximation.

Citation target:
Senovilla, "General electric-magnetic decomposition of fields," arXiv:gr-qc/0010095, or equivalent standard reference.

3. Isaacson effective stress-energy

Isaacson's high-frequency gravitational-wave construction provides an averaged, approximately gauge-invariant effective stress-energy tensor for gravitational waves.

Atlas implication:
Isaacson is an ordinary-energy comparison channel for high-frequency gravitational radiation. It is not identical to the finite-domain curvature-square ledger.

Citation target:
Isaacson, "Gravitational radiation in the limit of high frequency. II. Nonlinear terms and the effective stress tensor," Phys. Rev. 166, 1272.

4. Bondi-Sachs / News

Bondi-Sachs formalism gives the asymptotic radiation framework in which mass loss due to gravitational radiation is represented at null infinity through News/mass-loss structure.

Atlas implication:
Bondi/News belongs to the wave-zone/asymptotic validation lane, not to the near-zone finite-domain `B_E` ledger.

Citation target:
Madler and Winicour, "Bondi-Sachs Formalism," Scholarpedia / Living Reviews-style review, arXiv:1609.01731.

5. `Psi_4` / radiation extraction

Numerical relativity commonly extracts gravitational waves using the Newman-Penrose scalar `Psi_4` on extraction worldtubes or at null infinity.

Atlas implication:
`Psi_4` is a radiative-Weyl witness channel. It can compare to C5-B radiative structure but does not make the static C5-A electric ledger radiative.

Citation target:
Bishop and Rezzolla, "Extraction of gravitational waves in numerical relativity," Living Reviews in Relativity 2016.
