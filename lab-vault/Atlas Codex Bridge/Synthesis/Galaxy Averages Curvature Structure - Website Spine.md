# Galaxy Averages Curvature Structure - Website Spine

## Working Title

When Galaxy Averages Hide Curvature Structure

## Short Deck

Atlas ran a controlled sequence of toy-galaxy simulations to test a simple question: when astronomers compress a many-source rotating galaxy into common coarse-grained summaries, what survives, and what is lost?

The early answer is precise enough to be interesting and cautious enough to be honest. Scalar rotation summaries can remain comparatively well behaved while the local electric-Weyl tensor structure and eigenframe orientation seen by relational witnesses change much more strongly.

This is not a dark-matter claim. It is not a challenge to GR or SR. It is a computational-methods result inside the standard weak-field/GR-compatible language of curvature, witnesses, and averaging.

## Core Claim Shape

Standard coarse-graining choices can preserve useful scalar rotation information while materially altering the local electric-Weyl/eigenframe structure seen by relational witnesses.

## Public Summary

Atlas has begun a controlled study of mesoscale curvature loss in galaxy-scale coarse-graining. In toy Milky-Way-like simulations, common averaging methods can retain recognizable scalar rotation behavior while substantially altering the local electric-Weyl tensor and eigenframe structure seen by relational witnesses.

The result is not a claim about dark matter or new physics. It is a methods result about what information survives averaging.
