================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The gravitational coherence surface formalism defines a boundary of
source-readable gravitational distinguishability. The many-source parity
construction extends that idea by comparing source-readable weak-field
contributions pairwise. A remaining question is operational: can derived
readability structures be extracted from many-source weak-field configurations
under an explicit comparison rule?

This paper reports three proof-of-concept extraction results using the tidal
readability proxy Q_i proportional to M_i/r_i^3. First, in an extreme-ratio
configuration, a finite local readable pocket remains recoverable around a
subordinate source inside a globally dominant environment. Second, in a dense
many-source configuration, retained boundary-support samples assemble into a
foam-like interface morphology generated by source-readable competition rather
than imposed tessellation. Third, in matched-budget source ensembles, smooth,
weak-arm, and lopsided arrangements produce non-degenerate boundary-support
responses.

The results are interpreted conservatively. They do not imply modified gravity,
a new force, a physical boundary, a new invariant, or direct observability. They
establish a narrower threshold claim: under a declared weak-field readability
rule, many-source gravitational scenes can yield operationally recoverable
mesoscale boundary-support structure whose morphology responds to hierarchy,
density, and source arrangement.

Keywords:
weak-field gravity, gravitational readability, parity extraction,
many-source gravity, boundary-support geometry, mesoscale structure,
morphology sensitivity

================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The gravitational coherence surface program begins with a descriptive question:
where does a source-specific gravitational signal remain readable against its
environment? The many-source parity construction extends that question from
source-environment comparison to source-source comparison. In that setting,
pairwise equality surfaces and retained parity networks provide a mathematical
language for source-readable balance.

Definitions alone, however, are not enough. A parity network that cannot be
extracted is only a formal possibility. A proposed mesoscale readability layer
that cannot be harvested, visualized, perturbed, or compared remains a metaphor.

This paper therefore asks a narrower operational question:

    Can derived readability structures be recovered from many-source weak-field
    configurations under a declared comparison rule?

The answer reported here is yes, in a proof-of-concept sense. The examples are
limited, and their interpretation is deliberately conservative. They establish
recoverability, not final invariance or observational status.

================================================================================
2. SCOPE AND CLAIM DISCIPLINE
================================================================================

All results in this paper are interpreted within standard weak-field general
relativity. No modification of gravity is proposed.

The declared readable branch is the scalar tidal proxy

    Q_i(x) proportional to M_i / r_i(x)^3.

Given sources i and j, the comparison field is

    Psi_ij(x) = Q_i(x) - Q_j(x).

Boundary-support samples are retained where the declared comparison rule
identifies parity, low-margin structure, or related support behavior. The
figures are visual presentations of extracted support structures, not the source
of the result itself.

This paper does not claim that the reported structures are physical membranes,
force barriers, geodesic tracks, new invariants, or direct observables. It also
does not claim that every possible readable branch produces the same structure.

The claim is operational: under the declared rule, structured support geometry
can be recovered and compared across source configurations.

================================================================================
3. MINIMAL EXTRACTION SETUP
================================================================================

The proof-of-concept results use exact-source weak-field evaluations. Source
contributions are computed from the declared source ensemble rather than from an
imposed tessellation or coarse-grained density field. The readable contribution
for source i follows the branch

    Q_i proportional to M_i / r_i^3.

The extraction logic is support-first. The primary objects are retained support
samples and low-margin structures satisfying the declared comparison rule.
Rendered point clouds or surfaces are downstream presentations of those supports.

Three contexts are examined:

1. an extreme-ratio local-pocket test;
2. a dense many-source boundary-support harvest;
3. a matched-budget morphology comparison.

These three contexts test hierarchy, emergence, and arrangement sensitivity.

================================================================================
4. RESULT 1: EXTREME-RATIO LOCAL READABLE POCKET
================================================================================

The first test examines whether a subordinate source can retain a local readable
support region inside a globally dominant environment.

If extraction simply followed the largest contribution everywhere, the output
would collapse into a trivial single-source wash. Instead, the extracted support
shows a finite local pocket around the subordinate source, compressed and
displaced by the dominant competitor.

The important point is not that the pocket is a physical wall. It is not. The
point is that local readability is not erased merely because the broader scene
is strongly hierarchical. The support remains finite, anisotropic, and shaped by
the larger environment.

This establishes the first proof-of-concept criterion: persistence under
hierarchy.

================================================================================
5. RESULT 2: DENSE MANY-SOURCE BOUNDARY-SUPPORT ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

The second test asks whether dense many-source competition yields extractable
support architecture at all.

Under the declared readable comparison rule, the retained support samples form a
foam-like interface morphology. The term "foam-like" is visual, not ontological.
No physical foam or microscopic structure is implied. The recovered morphology
indicates that interface-bearing support structure can emerge from
source-readable competition rather than being imposed as a prior tessellation.

This establishes the second proof-of-concept criterion: emergence under
many-source complexity.

================================================================================
6. RESULT 3: MATCHED-BUDGET MORPHOLOGY SENSITIVITY
================================================================================

The third test asks whether the extracted support geometry responds to source
arrangement rather than only to total source budget.

Three source ensembles with matched exact-source budgets are compared: a smooth
distribution, a weak-arm distribution, and a lopsided distribution. The retained
support patterns are not degenerate. The smooth case gives a more even support
response. The weak-arm case develops directional extension. The lopsided case
shows stronger imbalance and preferential concentration.

[Insert Figure 3: Morphology sensitivity under matched source budget]

This does not yet define a morphology invariant or a classification metric. It
does show that the support geometry responds to spatial arrangement in a way
that is visible under matched-budget conditions.

This establishes the third proof-of-concept criterion: sensitivity to
morphology.

================================================================================
7. CROSS-RESULT SYNTHESIS
================================================================================

The three results form a minimal evidence triad.

The extreme-ratio pocket shows that local readable structure can persist under
strong hierarchy.

The dense many-source harvest shows that support geometry can emerge in complex
source environments.

The matched-budget comparison shows that the recovered structure responds to
arrangement, not merely to total source count or budget.

Together, these results establish operational recoverability under the declared
weak-field readability rule. That is the appropriate threshold claim for a first
results paper.

================================================================================
8. LIMITS
================================================================================

The present results are preliminary.

They do not establish branch-independent uniqueness. A different readable
operator may produce different support geometry.

They do not establish observational status. The extracted structures are derived
diagnostics, not direct measurements.

They do not establish invariance. Stability under perturbation, sampling,
domain, and branch changes remains to be tested systematically.

They do not establish a new physical object. The supports are induced structures
inside a declared comparison rule.

They do not establish a final extraction standard. Certification criteria must
be hardened in subsequent benchmark work.

================================================================================
9. NEXT PHASE
================================================================================

The next phase is benchmark hardening.

The equal-mass and unequal-mass two-source cases should be treated as analytic
anchors. The equal-mass three-source case should serve as the first nontrivial
many-source benchmark. Perturbed and unequal configurations should test
continuous deformation. Dense configurations should be tested for persistence
under refinement and source-roster changes.

The scalar branch Q_i proportional to M_i/r_i^3 should remain the baseline until
it is stable. Richer weak-field tidal tensor projections or norm-based branches
can then be compared against it.

Only after stability is demonstrated should the results be correlated with
downstream tasks such as trajectory comparison, residual analysis, or
morphology-sensitive classification.

================================================================================
10. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The coherence-surface and many-source parity constructions define a diagnostic
language for gravitational readability. This paper shows that the language can
be made operational in first proof-of-concept form.

Under the declared weak-field readable rule Q_i proportional to M_i/r_i^3,
extraction recovers finite local support under hierarchy, dense interface
architecture under many-source competition, and non-degenerate response under
matched-budget morphology variation.

These results do not change gravity. They do not define a new observable or
force. They show something narrower and useful: many-source weak-field scenes
can contain recoverable derived support geometry that deserves stricter
benchmark testing.

The next task is not expansion. It is hardening.

APPENDIX A. MINIMAL METHOD AND CONVENTIONS
================================================================================

All examples use standard weak-field source evaluation under the declared scalar
readable branch

    Q_i proportional to M_i / r_i^3.

Pairwise comparison is represented by

    Psi_ij = Q_i - Q_j.

Retained supports are downstream of the declared comparison rule. Rendered
figures should be read as visualizations of extracted supports, not as the
extraction itself.
