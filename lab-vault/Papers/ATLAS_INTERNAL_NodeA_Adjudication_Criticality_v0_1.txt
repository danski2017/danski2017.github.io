Foundation alignment

The current public and private roots define the required grammar.

Paper I inheritance

A Gravitational Coherence Surface is a parity horizon where a declared gravitational contribution reaches equality with its derived gravitational context under a declared readability rule. It is not a causal horizon, event horizon, material membrane, force boundary, or orbital stability limit.

This note must therefore avoid older root language in which the GCS is defined as parity with a ``calibrated environmental floor.'' Calibration may be a measurement-layer special case, but it is not the root definition.

Paper II inheritance

The many-source grammar is
[
(x)=(x)-(x),
=x:(x)=0.
]
Retained portions of pairwise parity surfaces may assemble into many-source parity networks. An adjudication candidate, if it exists, must be derived from this retained GCS/parity-context structure. It must not replace it.

Paper III inheritance

Paper III gives the interpretive layer: retained faces, seams, low-margin regions, and transition loci are derived interface geometry in the mesoscale comparison layer. The adjudication hypothesis belongs downstream of that layer. It asks what happens when such derived geometry approaches critical reclassification.

Paper IV inheritance

Paper IV establishes only proof-of-concept recoverability under the declared scalar tidal-readable branch. It does not establish invariance, observability, or final extraction standards. This note must inherit that restraint. Adjudication candidates must be treated as proposed diagnostics requiring stricter benchmark testing.

Core hypothesis

Internal working claim

A retained GCS or many-source parity-context structure may tolerate large geometric deformation while remaining one lawful contextual enclosure. A would-be perforation, fission, or closure transition may instead localize into a critical site before the geometry can be lawfully reclassified. That site is a Node A candidate or adjudication candidate.

Definition

An adjudication event is a hypothesized localized critical transition in derived readability geometry where a retained GCS/parity-context structure can no longer preserve a single coherent contextual assignment under deformation.

It is not:

 a curvature singularity,
 a force center,
 a physical source,
 an event horizon or trapping horizon,
 a geodesic attractor,
 an observable by itself,
 evidence of spacetime discreteness,
 a canonical Phase I result.
itemize

It is a candidate diagnostic site where topology, closure, or contextual assignment may change under a declared readability rule.

Adjudication ladder

The useful ladder is:

enumerate
 Smooth deformation. A retained boundary bends, stretches, corrugates, or compresses while preserving its class.
 Critical constriction. A neck or pinch forms. The geometry approaches possible reclassification.
 Nodal condensation. A localized Node A candidate appears as unresolved readable obligations concentrate.
 Reseal. The boundary returns to its prior class after the stress passes.
 Fission. The structure separates into multiple lawful descendant cells.
 Higher-genus continuation. A true multiply connected retained object persists. This is rare and requires strong evidence.
enumerate

This ladder is conceptual until benchmarked. It must not be used as evidence by itself.

Evidence requirements

A Node A candidate earns interpretation only if it passes tests aligned with the architecture canon:

enumerate

No render may promote a Node A candidate by itself.

Benchmark program

The benchmark program should proceed in increasing difficulty.

Stage 1: analytic anchors

Use equal-mass two-source, unequal-mass two-source, and equal-mass equilateral three-source scenes to verify that no false Node A candidates appear where benchmark geometry is simple.

Stage 2: controlled three-source deformation

Perturb one mass or one position in the equilateral scene. Track retained faces, seams, low-margin regions, and candidate constrictions. Require continuous deformation before any criticality claim.

Stage 3: four-source stratification test

Use a controlled four-source scene to test face, triple-junction, and possible quadruple-junction behavior. Look for codimension typing, but keep it descriptive until stable.

Stage 4: threshold-family sweeps

Sweep $$ in $C_i()$ and measure component count, Euler characteristic where practical, Betti numbers where available, closure score, and candidate critical thresholds.

Stage 5: artifact rejection

Repeat across sampling, domain, threshold, and rendering styles. A candidate that disappears under modest changes is an artifact or unstable scratch result.

Stage 6: optional eigenstructure bridge

Only after stable GCS/parity-context criticality appears, compare with tidal tensor eigenstructure diagnostics. Eigenstructure may support Node A candidacy but must not replace GCS evidence.
