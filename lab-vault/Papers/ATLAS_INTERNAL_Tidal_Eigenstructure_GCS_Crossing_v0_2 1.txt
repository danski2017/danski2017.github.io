================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

This synchronized internal note preserves the viable core of the earlier tidal-organization
lab paper while repairing its terminology, claim boundary, and architecture alignment.

The working hypothesis is narrow but important: a GCS crossing does not create a force
discontinuity, physical barrier, causal horizon, orbital-stability boundary, or modified
dynamics. A point-particle center-of-mass trajectory remains smooth. However, for an
extended body whose internal orientation and rotational response depend on the tidal
tensor, a crossing of a retained GCS/parity-context boundary may correlate with a sharp
change in the organization of the local tidal eigenstructure. In favorable geometries, that
eigenstructure transition may generate a nonzero reorientation torque on an elongated
body.

The motivating source paper used an idealized Earth-Moon-Sun thought experiment and a
simplified rubble-pile asteroid model. It reported a large transient excursion in a
tidal-alignment diagnostic near the adopted shell crossing, a peak order-of-magnitude
reorientation torque near 1.77 x 10^4 N m, and a benchmark transit-scale reorientation of
about 17 degrees under the chosen geometry and body model. These values are not universal
constants, not observational claims, and not canonical evidence. They are internal
result-seeds that motivate a stricter validation program.

The synchronized claim is therefore:

A derived GCS/parity-context crossing may mark a tidal eigenstructure reorganization
event for extended bodies, and that event may be testable through rotational diagnostics
or population statistics once ordinary confounders are controlled.

This is a live internal hypothesis, not a public result.

4. CORE HYPOTHESIS
================================================================================

Internal working claim:
In standard weak-field GR, a retained GCS/parity-context boundary may coincide with a rapid
change in the principal tidal eigenstructure experienced by an extended body. Because extended
bodies respond to tidal gradients through orientation, stress, deformation, and torque, such a
crossing may produce a nonzero rotational response even though the center-of-mass trajectory
remains smooth.

Compact statement:
The GCS does not push the body. The tidal tensor reorganizes across the retained boundary, and
an extended body may respond to that reorganization.

This is not a new force. It is a possible response to ordinary tidal-tensor structure.

6. DECLARED WEAK-FIELD SETUP
================================================================================

Let a declared weak-field scene contain physical sources S_i with masses M_i and positions x_i.

Let

    r_i(x) = ||x - x_i||,

or with softening,

    r_{i,eps}(x) = sqrt(||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2).

The Phase I scalar readable branch is

    Q_i(x) = M_i / (||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2)^(3/2),

or away from source locations,

    Q_i(x) proportional to M_i / r_i(x)^3.

Pairwise residuals are

    Psi_ij(x) = Q_i(x) - Q_j(x).

A retained GCS/parity-context boundary is a diagnostic support extracted from equality,
near-equality, closure, or contribution-context parity under declared rules.

The weak-field potential may be written

    Phi(x) = -G sum_i M_i / r_{i,eps}(x).

The weak-field tidal tensor is

    E_ab(x) = partial_a partial_b Phi(x),

and for a point source contribution away from the source,

    E_i(x) = G M_i (3 n_i n_i^T - I) / r_i^3,

where

    n_i = (x - x_i) / r_i.

The total tidal tensor is

    E_total(x) = sum_i E_i(x).

The eigenstructure is defined by

    E_total(x) v_k(x) = lambda_k(x) v_k(x).

The leading principal eigenvector v_1 and eigenvalue spread or shear provide the local tidal
orientation and strain diagnostics used in this branch.

================================================================================
7. TIDAL EIGENSTRUCTURE DIAGNOSTIC
================================================================================

A point particle responds to the local connection and follows the ordinary smooth trajectory
determined by the total field. An extended body also samples spatial derivatives across its
finite extent. Its orientation, stress, and torque response can depend on the tidal tensor.

Define a leading tidal direction

    v_1(x),

as the principal eigenvector of E_total(x), with sign convention fixed by continuity or by an
explicit source-facing orientation convention.

Define a declared reference direction associated with the dominant readable contribution, for
example

    e_dom(x) = unit vector from dominant source to x,

in a clean radial benchmark, or a richer reference in a tensor/projection branch.

Define an alignment diagnostic

    alpha(x) = arccos(|v_1(x) dot e_dom(x)|).

The absolute value removes eigenvector sign ambiguity. If the branch cares about signed
orientation, the sign convention must be declared separately.

Interpretation:

- small alpha: principal tidal eigenstructure aligns with the declared dominant readable reference;
- large alpha: the local tidal eigenstructure is contested, rotated, or not well described by the
  declared dominant radial source alone;
- rapid change in alpha along a trajectory: candidate tidal-eigenstructure reorganization.

This alpha is a diagnostic. It is not an invariant by itself and not an observable by default.

================================================================================
9. SOURCE RESULT SEEDS
================================================================================

The source note reported the following internal result seeds.

R1. Smooth force, sharp organization.
The total gravitational field remains smooth across the crossing. No force discontinuity is
introduced. Nevertheless, the tidal eigenstructure diagnostic can change sharply.

R2. Alignment excursion.
In the deliberately clean EMS geometry, the tidal-alignment diagnostic reached a large
transient excursion, reported as 90 degrees at the crossing under the adopted convention.
Synchronized interpretation: this is a geometry-dependent diagnostic maximum in a controlled
test, not a universal GCS-crossing constant.

R3. Torque estimate.
For the simplified elongated rubble-pile asteroid, the source note used the quadrupolar
tidal-torque estimate

    tau = 0.5 * Delta I * Delta lambda * sin(2 theta),

where Delta I is the relevant moment-of-inertia difference, Delta lambda is the eigenvalue
shear of the total tidal tensor, and theta is the body-axis misalignment with the principal
stretching direction.

The source benchmark reported

    Delta I = 1.48 x 10^17 kg m^2,
    Delta lambda = 1.19 x 10^-13 s^-2,
    peak tau approximately 1.77 x 10^4 N m.

R4. Transit-scale estimate.
The source note reported a reorientation timescale near 1038 hours and a shell-transit time
near 454 hours for an adopted 10 percent shell width, yielding a toy estimate of order
10 degrees per transit, with a benchmark value about 17 degrees.

Synchronized interpretation:
This number is a model-dependent internal estimate. It should motivate better rotational
dynamics, not be treated as a prediction.

R5. Nested hierarchy lesson.
The source note argued that Earth-Moon-Sun does not behave as one connected three-way foam
regime. It behaves as a decoupled nested hierarchy: Earth-Moon local parity at one scale,
Earth/Sun or Earth-parent support at another scale, solar/local-stellar context at a larger
scale. This aligns with child/parent doctrine when phrased carefully.

10. FORCE CONTINUITY VERSUS TIDAL ORGANIZATION
================================================================================

This branch depends on a strict separation:

Force continuity:
The total gravitational field and center-of-mass trajectory remain smooth. A point particle
does not encounter a wall, impulse, or discontinuity at the retained GCS/parity-context
boundary.

Tidal organization:
The eigenvectors and eigenvalues of E_total may change rapidly as the relative contribution
structure changes. An extended body with finite size and anisotropic moment of inertia may
experience torque because its internal alignment responds to E_total.

This distinction keeps the branch inside standard GR.

The boundary does not cause the torque as an independent object. The ordinary tidal tensor
causes the torque. The boundary may locate or correlate with the transition zone where the
organizing eigenstructure changes.

Preferred phrasing:
The retained GCS/parity-context boundary may act as a cartographic marker of tidal
eigenstructure reorganization.

Avoid:
The GCS exerts torque.

13. RELATION TO HILL SPHERE AND STABILITY BOUNDARIES
================================================================================

The Hill sphere and GCS answer different questions.

Hill sphere:
A dynamical stability scale in a rotating-frame restricted three-body approximation.

GCS / parity-context boundary:
A contribution-context or source-readable parity boundary under a declared readability rule.

Tidal-eigenstructure transition:
A possible change in the principal tidal organization experienced by an extended body.

These can occur at neighboring scales because the same mass hierarchy shapes them, but they are
not identical.

Safe hypothesis:
Readability boundaries and dynamical stability boundaries are distinct objects that may show
systematic correlations in nested weak-field systems because both are shaped by parent-child mass
hierarchy.

Unsafe claim:
GCS replaces Hill sphere.

17. FAILURE MODES
================================================================================

F1. Eigenframe rotation does not correlate with retained boundary crossing after better
trajectory and tensor evaluation.

F2. The large alignment excursion is only an artifact of reference-vector convention.

F3. Eigenvector rotation is caused by eigenvalue degeneracy and is numerically unstable.

F4. Torque estimates collapse under more realistic body orientation or spin-state modeling.

F5. Transit-scale reorientation becomes negligible compared with known effects under realistic
conditions.

F6. Population-test signal is erased by YORP, collisions, selection bias, or orbital-class
confounding.

F7. Boundary location changes too much across reasonable readability prescriptions.

F8. The branch encourages claim-rung skipping from diagnostic boundary to physical force.

If these failure modes dominate, preserve the note as an instructive thought experiment but do not
promote it.
