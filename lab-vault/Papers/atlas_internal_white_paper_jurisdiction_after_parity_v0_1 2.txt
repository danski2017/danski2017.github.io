1. Executive Summary

The earlier Paper VI direction, centered on parity stacks and an identity surface, failed because it allowed a readability-family or k-like operator to behave as an outward identity-expansion mechanism. That contradicted the root definition of a gravitational coherence surface.

A GCS is the surface where a declared gravitational contribution reaches equality with its derived context under a declared readability rule. Once contribution and context reach parity, the declared source no longer possesses clean unilateral attribution authority beyond that boundary. Therefore, any construction that expands source identity outward past the certified GCS is jurisdictionally invalid.

This white paper proposes a corrected internal attractor:

Jurisdiction After Parity.

The corrected idea does not ask whether identity can expand outward. It asks what becomes lawful after a GCS has already been certified.

The working attractor is:

A certified GCS may define a source-resolved attribution jurisdiction. Its interior may be mapped by recursive attribution bisection. Its rim may record contextual negotiation. The interpretability of both interior and rim structure depends on co-declared K-resolution and datum-resolution. Meaningful child sources may impose local resolution obligations. All identity language remains conditional on convergence and independent witness support.

This is not public doctrine. It is a lab attractor. It is allowed to guide experiments, language, scene design, and diagnostic design. It is not allowed to protect itself from failure.

If the branch fails monopole controls, K/datum refinement, convergence testing, or independent witness comparison, Atlas abandons it.


2. Background

The GCS sequence established the core public object: a gravitational coherence surface is a parity boundary where a declared contribution reaches equality with its derived context under a declared readability rule. The GCS is not a force cutoff, material membrane, causal horizon, or physical wall. It is a derived mathematical object inside a declared gravitational scene. The foundational grammar requires scene, contribution, derived context, and readability rule.

The many-source extension introduced pairwise source-readable comparisons, residual fields, retained parity faces, seams, and junctions. It also established a crucial discipline: not every algebraic zero set deserves interpretive rank. Retention and relevance matter.

The mesoscale readability paper framed parity faces, seams, low-margin regions, and transition loci as candidate derived interface geometry between pointwise field evaluation and coarse averaging. It emphasized persistence, perturbation testing, and failure conditions.

The proof-of-concept extraction paper showed that under the declared scalar tidal-readable branch, many-source weak-field scenes can yield recoverable boundary-support structure, while explicitly limiting the claim to operational recoverability rather than invariance, observability, or final physical authority.

The nesting handoff paper elevated scene declaration, parent context, child domains, handoff confidence, tidal orientation witnesses, and the distinction between acceleration dominance, tidal dominance, and identity dominance. It also defined gravitational scenes as structured declarations rather than mere coordinate containers.

The information-boundary draft proposed a speculative interpretation of GCS surfaces as source-attribution boundaries. In a normalized binary support model, attribution entropy peaks exactly at contribution-context parity. This provides a formal bridge between GCS parity and maximal attribution uncertainty, but only at a carefully limited claim level.

The failed Paper VI branch tried to extend these ideas through a parity-stack or identity-surface construction. That branch is retracted from public doctrine. The present white paper does not revive it. It extracts the usable attractor from its failure.


3. The Failure Mode of the Old Paper VI

The previous Paper VI formulation treated k-family variation as though it could reveal or expand a larger identity surface around a declared source.

That was the error.

The GCS is already the boundary at which a declared contribution reaches equality with its derived context. If a k-like branch is allowed to inflate source identity beyond that boundary, it violates the very parity condition that gives the GCS its meaning.

The failure was not the use of identity language.

The failure was outward jurisdictional inflation.

Identity language remains potentially useful if disciplined as source-attribution bookkeeping identity inside a declared scene. But identity cannot be allowed to behave as a mystical expansion field. It must inherit from certified parity, not create parity retroactively.

Internal correction:

No k-family, branch family, foliation parameter, render parameter, or interpretive layer may extend source-resolved jurisdiction beyond a certified GCS unless a new scene, contribution, context, and readability rule certify a new boundary.


4. The New Attractor: Jurisdiction After Parity

The new internal attractor is named:

Jurisdiction After Parity.

It begins only after a GCS has been certified.

The core question is no longer:

How far can identity extend?

The corrected question is:

Once contribution-context parity has certified a boundary, what lawful structure exists inside the source-resolved domain and at its contested rim?

The attractor has four parts:

First, a certified GCS defines a source-resolved jurisdiction.

Second, the interior of that jurisdiction may be mapped by recursive attribution bisection.

Third, the rim may be studied as a context-negotiation zone.

Fourth, K-resolution and datum-resolution must be co-declared before any interior or rim structure can be certified.

This attractor is not a claim of discovery. It is a disciplined experimental target.


5. Boundary Primacy

The GCS is sovereign.

It is not one shell among many. It is the first earned boundary.

A GCS is defined by a declared contribution, a derived context, and a readability rule. At the GCS, source and context reach equality. This gives the GCS boundary primacy.

Inside the certified boundary, the declared source has dominant attribution support over its derived context.

At the boundary, source and context are in parity.

Beyond the boundary, the source no longer holds clean unilateral attribution authority.

This gives the first internal axiom:

Axiom 1: Boundary Primacy

No interior, rim, wireframe, k-family, recursive layer, foam structure, or identity claim has interpretive authority unless it inherits from a certified GCS or from another explicitly certified parity relation.

7. Recursive Attribution Bisection

Interior structure should not be generated by arbitrary shell depths.

Once the GCS is certified, the interior may be foliated by recursive attribution bisection.

Let the declared source contribution be A and its derived context be K. Let their comparable readable supports be QA and QK.

Define attribution weights:

wA = QA / (QA + QK)

wK = QK / (QA + QK)

At the GCS:

wA = 1/2
wK = 1/2

The GCS is therefore the 50/50 attribution boundary.

Moving inward, the source attribution increases and the contextual attribution decreases. Instead of selecting arbitrary depths, define interior layers by recursively halving the remaining contextual claim.

Interior sequence:

GCS boundary:
50/50

First inward layer:
75/25

Second inward layer:
87.5/12.5

Third inward layer:
93.75/6.25

Fourth inward layer:
96.875/3.125

Equivalent formulation:

wK = 2^-n
wA = 1 - 2^-n

where n = 1, 2, 3, ...

The GCS is n = 1.

Each layer halves the remaining contextual attribution claim.

These layers are not physical shells. They are bookkeeping strata.

Axiom 2: Recursive Interior

Inside a certified GCS, attribution strata must be generated by recursive bisection or another declared non-arbitrary rule. They must not be hand-picked for visual effect.

9. Monopole Control

An ideal monopole should be quiet.

If a declared source is represented as a smooth monopole and its derived context is smooth, recursive bisection should produce smooth nested attribution strata. No rich skeleton, internal foam, branch structure, or seam network should appear.

This is not a failure.

It is the correct null.

Earlier wireframe attempts may have looked disappointing because they were asking a smooth ideal monopole to produce internal structure it did not possess. In that case, smoothness is evidence of method honesty.

Axiom 3: Monopole Null

A clean monopole with smooth context should produce smooth interior bisection and no internal skeleton. A complex wireframe in such a control is a warning sign, not a triumph.

13. Datum Duality

Datum-resolution is the dual axis of K-resolution.

K-resolution determines how finely context can speak.

Datum-resolution determines how finely the scene can be heard.

A finely foliated K cannot be certified by a sparse datum.

A coarse K cannot support fine foam claims unless the analysis is explicitly an audit for missing structure.

A high-resolution datum applied to a smooth K should report smoothness, not invent complexity.

This yields the resolution-matching principle:

Certification requires K/datum resolution matching.

Examples:

Smooth K + high-resolution datum:
expected result is smoothness unless the high-resolution audit reveals that K was underdeclared.

Rostered K + sparse datum:
uncertified foam or missed structure likely.

Nested K + no child-associated datums:
handoff claims are under-witnessed.

Uncertainty-bounded K + no confidence ledger:
false precision risk.

Axiom 7: Datum Duality

K-resolution and datum-resolution must be co-declared. No structure may be certified at a finer scale than both K and the datum can support.


14. Meaningful Source Resolution Obligation

The smallest object in a scene does not automatically set global resolution.

That would make the method impossible.

Instead, the smallest meaningful declared source creates a local resolution obligation.

A source is meaningful when it has interpretive rank in the claim being tested.

A source may be meaningful because:

it is part of the declared contribution
it is part of the derived context
it participates in source-source parity
it participates in child-parent handoff
it affects a morphology-sensitive comparison
it is the smallest declared source whose identity is being tested

Best-practice rule:

Meaningful sources set local resolution obligations.
Convergence tests decide whether those obligations were satisfied.

EMS example:

If the question is Earth monopole readability in solar context, Moon-local resolution may not be required.

If the question is Earth-Moon nesting in solar context, Moon-scale resolution becomes locally obligatory.

If the question is Moon-local identity or handoff, Moon-associated datums and Moon-scale K-foliation are required.

Axiom 8: Meaningful-Source Obligation

The smallest meaningful source creates local resolution obligations where claims involve that source, but does not impose global maximum resolution by default.
