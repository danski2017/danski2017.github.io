================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

This internal note synchronizes the earlier white paper "Toward a Manifold Gravity Calculus" with current Atlas vocabulary, lane governance, and claim discipline. The viable core is not a new gravitational theory and not a public claim that Atlas has established a new calculus. The viable core is an internal grammar for derived diagnostic structures extracted from a declared weak-field many-source scene: GCS/parity-context cells, retained faces, seams, junctions, low-margin regions, handoff supports, offset families, nested in-cell leaves, child-datum candidates, and lineage-preserving scaffold graphs.

The earlier phrase "manifold gravity calculus" is retained only as a developmental label. The synchronized internal term is recursive scaffold calculus: a support-first diagnostic grammar for how retained GCS/parity-context structures may be refined, related, integrated, and propagated without modifying general relativity. Physical sources generate the modeled field. The certified relational ledger stores the lawful state. Node Psi extracts GCS/parity-context structures. Validation determines whether those structures earn interpretation. Rendering communicates but never decides.

This note does not modify Einstein's equations, introduce a new force, replace differential geometry, define a physical scaffold, or claim that derived parity structures are observables by themselves. It proposes a private Atlas method branch for organizing derived readability geometry after canonical Phase I extraction has been stabilized.

5. FORMAL STARTING POINT
================================================================================

Let a declared weak-field scene be evaluated on a chosen spatial slice Sigma_t. Let physical sources S_i have masses M_i and positions x_i. The current Phase I scalar readable branch is

    Q_i(x) = M_i / (||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2)^(3/2),

or, away from sources in analytic shorthand,

    Q_i(x) proportional to M_i / r_i(x)^3.

The pairwise residual is

    Psi_ij(x) = Q_i(x) - Q_j(x).

The pairwise parity surface is

    Sigma_ij = { x in U : Psi_ij(x) = 0 },

where U is the declared comparison domain.

The source-readable margin against the strongest local competitor may be written

    m_i(x) = Q_i(x) - max_{j != i} Q_j(x).

A margin-threshold in-cell family may be written

    C_i(tau) = { x : m_i(x) >= tau }.

These definitions are diagnostic. Q_i is not the metric. Psi_ij is not a force. Sigma_ij is not a wall. C_i(tau) is not a physical enclosure unless a separate physical interpretation is earned, which is not claimed here.

7. CANDIDATE OPERATORS
================================================================================

The earlier paper proposed bisection, offset, thickness, recursive support propagation, scaffold derivatives, and scaffold integrals. The synchronized internal grammar keeps these as candidate operators, not canonical methods.

O1. Parity extraction / bisection.
Given Q_i and Q_j, extract a retained support from Psi_ij = 0. This is not arbitrary bisection of space; it is field-earned equality under the declared readable rule.

O2. Relevance filtering.
From raw Sigma_ij candidates, retain only the portions that participate in local partition, transition, closure, or contextual parity structure under declared criteria.

O3. Thickness / low-margin expansion.
For declared epsilon,

    B_ij^epsilon = { x : |Psi_ij(x)| < epsilon }.

This identifies a near-parity neighborhood. It is a diagnostic tolerance band, not a physical membrane.

O4. Offset measurement.
Measure displacement of a retained support relative to a declared reference: equal-mass benchmark, previous run, previous threshold leaf, geometric bisector, barycentric reference, or parent-context baseline. The reference must be declared.

O5. Handoff extraction.
Inside overlap-active regions, identify local identity-transition supports using residual sign change, winner-change, low margin, or declared handoff criteria.

O6. Nested in-cell continuation.
For retained cells, threshold the margin field m_i(x) to produce C_i(tau), inner leaves, centroid trajectory c_i(tau), and core set K_i.

O7. Child-datum candidate propagation.
Validated support objects may propose child-datum candidates only if they preserve invertible registration back to Node 0 and explicit lineage back to the certified ledger.

O8. Scaffold graph assembly.
Assemble cells, faces, seams, junction candidates, handoff supports, in-cell leaves, and child-datum candidates into a lineage-aware graph for diagnostics and rendering.

O9. Validation.
Every operator must be tested against tolerance, sampling, domain, source-roster, and simpler-baseline checks.

9. CANDIDATE SCAFFOLD DERIVATIVES
================================================================================

The source paper proposed a handoff derivative. In synchronized form this remains a candidate diagnostic, not a new physical derivative.

Let n_ij be a declared local normal to a retained face I_ij, and let f be a declared scalar diagnostic field. A candidate transverse scaffold derivative may be written

    H_ij[f] = n_ij dot grad f evaluated on I_ij.

Possible uses:

- measure sharpness of readable handoff;
- compare transition stiffness across faces;
- identify strong low-margin gradients;
- support Node A candidate diagnostics when paired with stability checks;
- compare deformation across parameter sweeps.

Caution:
H_ij[f] does not replace ordinary derivatives on the manifold. It is a diagnostic derivative evaluated on an extracted support structure. It has meaning only after f, I_ij, n_ij, units, tolerance, and branch status are declared.

Related candidate derivatives:

- seam tangent derivative;
- face offset derivative under a deformation parameter;
- centroid trajectory derivative dc_i/dtau for in-cell families;
- closure-score derivative under source-roster or domain sweeps;
- eigenstructure rotation rate near retained supports, if tidal-eigenstructure branch is enabled.

    I_scaf[f] = sum_i int_{C_i} f dV

              + beta sum_{i<j<k} int_{Gamma_ijk} f ds
              + gamma sum_J f(J),

where C_i are retained cells, I_ij retained faces, Gamma_ijk seam supports, and J junction candidates. The weights alpha, beta, gamma must be declared and cannot be treated as universal constants.

Possible internal diagnostics:

This is not an action, not a conservation law, and not an observable unless a later empirical calibration earns that role.

14. TEST PROGRAM
================================================================================

Stage 1: canonical benchmark compatibility.
The scaffold grammar must reproduce Paper II benchmarks: equal-mass midpoint parity, unequal-mass one-third law, and equal-mass equilateral triple parity without inventing extra structures.

Stage 2: relevance filtering.
Test whether scaffold-state records improve retained-face selection relative to naive all-pairs extraction.

Stage 3: low-margin stability.
Sweep epsilon in B_ij^epsilon and test whether low-margin structures deform continuously rather than teleporting.

Stage 4: handoff branch comparison.
Compare closure-overlap-handoff structures to retained parity faces and determine whether handoff adds stable diagnostic value.

Stage 5: in-cell continuation.
For retained cells, compute C_i(tau), V_i(tau), A_i(tau), c_i(tau), and K_i. Compare against omni-radial and centroid/barycenter baselines.

Stage 6: scaffold functional sanity.
Compute simple functionals such as retained face area, seam length, low-margin volume, and closure-cell volume. Require declared units and normalization.

Stage 7: Node A guardrail.
Verify that criticality diagnostics do not produce false Node A candidates in simple benchmark scenes.

Stage 8: downstream correlation, optional.
Only after stability is demonstrated, test whether scaffold diagnostics correlate with geodesic comparisons, residual analysis, or morphology-sensitive classification.

15. FAILURE MODES
================================================================================

F1. The scaffold grammar adds vocabulary but no measurable diagnostic improvement.

F2. Candidate derivatives or functionals are unstable under modest tolerance or sampling changes.

F3. Child-datum propagation creates unregistered local patchwork.

F4. Low-margin bands become arbitrary tolerance artifacts.

F5. Handoff supports or scaffold graphs do not improve relevance filtering or interpretation.

F6. Rendered scaffolds look persuasive while machine-readable diagnostics fail.

F7. The grammar encourages claim-rung skipping from derived structure to dynamics, observability, or new physics.

If these failure modes dominate, this note should remain a conceptual fossil rather than a method branch.
