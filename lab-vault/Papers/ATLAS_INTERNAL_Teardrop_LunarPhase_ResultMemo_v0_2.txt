ABSTRACT
================================================================================

This internal memo preserves and synchronizes a major Atlas result night: the extraction of two related weak-field many-source readability signatures.

First, in a staged twelve-source scene, source-local closure clouds exhibit measurable asymmetry. The closure-support cloud of a source is displaced and elongated according to its relational competition with neighboring sources. This effect is here called the teardrop closure signature. Its first quantitative descriptors are the bias fraction, defined as closure-cloud centroid offset divided by mean closure radius, and the principal-axis aspect ratio of the closure cloud.

Second, in an Earth-Moon-Sun-Jupiter scene sampled across lunar phase, the Moon-associated handoff support shows a robust primary anti-Earth orientation and a secondary Sun-correlated phase modulation. Dense directionally-adapted sampling reveals this secondary modulation more cleanly than sparse isotropic sampling.

These are derived diagnostic signatures inside a declared weak-field readability branch. They are not forces, physical membranes, causal horizons, orbital-stability limits, new invariants, or direct observables by themselves. They are candidate Atlas diagnostics requiring convergence testing, branch comparison, roster sensitivity checks, and ledger-confirmed reproducibility before any public-facing or canonical claim.

4. DECLARED READABLE BRANCH
================================================================================

Let a declared weak-field source ensemble contain physical sources S_i with masses M_i and positions x_i.

For source i, define softened distance

    r_{i,eps}(x) = sqrt(||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2).

The current Phase I scalar readable branch is

    Q_i(x) = M_i / (||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2)^(3/2).

The source paper used the equivalent tidal-proxy scaling with a constant factor:

    Q_i(x) = 2 G M_i / |x - x_i|^3.

Since the factor 2G is common to all sources in pairwise equality, it cancels for parity and ordering diagnostics. Current Atlas notation should use the simpler canonical branch unless physical units require the constant factor.

Pairwise residuals are

    Psi_ij(x) = Q_i(x) - Q_j(x).

The dominant source at x is

    i*(x) = argmax_i Q_i(x).

A handoff support is a retained local transition structure where source-readable dominance or near-dominance changes under the declared extraction rule.

5. RESULT SEED 1: TEARDROP CLOSURE ASYMMETRY
================================================================================

The twelve-source staged cluster in the source paper contained one heavy source, three mid-tier sources, and eight small-tier sources on an AU-scale irregular layout.

The source paper reports a full-scene xy slice in which red closure-support points and blue first-handoff points form a cellular nested architecture around the staged source ensemble. The page 3 figure is important as a visual lineage marker: it shows that closure and handoff supports were not appearing as shapeless all-pairs clutter, but as organized scene-dependent structure.

For each source, the closure cloud was analyzed in source-relative coordinates using:

1. mean closure radius,
2. closure-cloud centroid offset,
3. bias fraction,
4. principal-axis aspect ratio.

Define closure-cloud centroid

    c_i = mean_x(x in closure support for source i).

Define source position

    x_i.

Define centroid offset vector

    b_i = c_i - x_i.

Define mean closure radius

    r_mean,i = mean_x ||x - x_i||.

Define bias fraction

    beta_i = ||b_i|| / r_mean,i.

A perfectly isotropic sampled closure cloud would have beta_i approximately 0, subject to sampling noise.

The source paper reports the following pattern:

- heavy source S01: bias fraction 0.081;
- mid-tier sources S02-S04: bias fractions approximately 0.267 to 0.296;
- small-tier sources: variable bias fractions approximately 0.106 to 0.267;
- aspect ratios ranging roughly 1.51 to 2.35.

Interpretation:
The heavy source appears comparatively uncontested and therefore has lower closure centroid bias. Mid-tier sources are more strongly shaped by the heavy source and show larger closure asymmetry. Small-tier sources show context-dependent deformation based on their local competitive environment.

This is the teardrop closure signature.

Current claim:
The teardrop closure signature is a candidate derived diagnostic of source-in-scene competition. It measures the asymmetry of a retained closure-support cloud under a declared readability branch.

It is not yet an observable in the public-science sense.

6. RESULT SEED 2: LUNAR-PHASE HANDOFF MODULATION
================================================================================

The source paper next examined an Earth-Moon-Sun-Jupiter scene across 36 lunar phases.

For each phase, the extraction pipeline identified Moon-associated handoff support and computed its centroid relative to the Moon.

Let H_M(phi) be the Moon-associated handoff support at lunar phase phi.

Define handoff centroid

    h_M(phi) = mean_x(x in H_M(phi)).

Define Moon position

    x_M(phi).

Define handoff bias vector

    u_M(phi) = h_M(phi) - x_M(phi).

The source paper compares u_M(phi) against reference directions:

- toward Earth,
- anti-Earth,
- toward Sun,
- anti-Sun.

Primary signal:
Across the 36 phases, the Moon-associated handoff centroid points close to the anti-Earth direction. The source paper reports approximately 4.4 degrees ± 2.9 degrees from anti-Earth.

Interpretation:
Earth is the dominant local competitor for Moon-readable dominance. The Moon-associated handoff support is compressed or concentrated toward Earth-facing transition regions, while the centroid offset indicates a stable anti-Earth bias in the support geometry.

Secondary signal:
Dense directionally-adapted sampling reveals a Sun-correlated modulation of the Moon-associated handoff bias over lunar phase. Sparse isotropic sampling shows the same candidate structure only noisily or incompletely.

The page 9 figure is the key result seed. Its upper panel compares the Moon handoff-bias direction against anti-Sun and anti-Earth directions across lunar phase. Its lower panel shows handoff-bias magnitude variation. The dense sampling path produces a cleaner phase-coherent curve than the sparse sampling path.

Current claim:
The lunar-phase result is a candidate extracted geometric modulation of Moon-associated handoff support, correlated with changing Sun-Earth-Moon geometry.

It is not yet a public claim of a direct observable.

9. REQUIRED HARDENING TESTS
================================================================================

A. Teardrop closure asymmetry tests

1. Equal isolated source sanity check.
   A single source or sufficiently isolated source should yield near-zero bias fraction, within sampling error.

2. Equal-mass two-source benchmark.
   Closure asymmetry should respect symmetry and not invent directional bias inconsistent with the scene.

3. Unequal-mass two-source benchmark.
   Bias direction and magnitude should deform smoothly with mass ratio.

4. Three-source equilateral benchmark.
   Symmetric source placement should suppress spurious asymmetric bias.

5. Staged cluster rerun.
   Reproduce the twelve-source result with current ledger and output standards.

6. Sampling sweep.
   Test beta_i and aspect ratio under increasing angular and radial resolution.

7. Domain sweep.
   Confirm that beta_i does not change materially under reasonable domain expansion.

8. Tolerance sweep.
   Confirm that extracted closure-cloud metrics deform continuously under extraction tolerance changes.

9. Branch comparison.
   Compare scalar Q_i results against tensor-norm or projection branches as validation only.

B. Lunar-phase handoff tests

1. Phase resolution sweep.
   Repeat 36, 72, and possibly 144 phase samples.

2. Angular sampling sweep.
   Compare sparse isotropic, dense isotropic, directionally-adapted, and omni-radial sampling.

3. Radial sampling sweep.
   Confirm that handoff centroid direction and magnitude stabilize with radial refinement.

4. Jupiter on/off roster sensitivity.
   Quantify whether Jupiter affects the modulation or is negligible at declared tolerance.

5. Sun off control.
   Remove Sun or replace with declared null parent context to verify loss of Sun-correlated modulation.

6. Inclination control.
   Compare zero-inclination and 5.14-degree inclined lunar orbit.

7. Coordinate-frame check.
   Verify that modulation is not an artifact of projection or frame convention.

8. Handoff criterion comparison.
   Compare winner-change, Psi near-zero, and low-margin definitions.

9. Ledger/render agreement.
   Ensure plotted curves are generated from machine-readable handoff records, not from render-only selection.

12. INTERNAL VALUE
================================================================================

This memo is worth keeping because it contains three durable advances:

1. Quantification.
   Closure clouds were not merely rendered. They were measured by bias fraction and aspect ratio.

2. Parameter dependence.
   The Moon-associated handoff support was tracked across orbital phase, turning extraction into a scene-parameter diagnostic.

3. Sampling insight.
   The lunar result exposed that sparse isotropic sampling can miss hierarchy-localized structure, while targeted/adaptive sampling can reveal it.

The third point must be handled carefully. Serious Atlas extraction still prefers omni-radial or validated unbiased interrogation. But adaptive sampling is valuable as a diagnostic flashlight when explicitly labeled and cross-checked.
