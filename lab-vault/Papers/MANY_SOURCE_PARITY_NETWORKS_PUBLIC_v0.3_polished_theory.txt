================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The gravitational coherence surface formalism defines an operational boundary of
source-specific gravitational distinguishability against a calibrated
environment. This paper develops the corresponding many-source construction. For
a weak-field source ensemble, assign each source a declared source-readable
tidal contribution Q_i. Pairwise equality, Q_i = Q_j, defines a balance surface
between sources. In the point-mass weak-field specialization Q_i proportional to
M_i/r_i^3, this construction recovers the midpoint plane for equal masses and
the deformation law r_i/r_j = (M_i/M_j)^(1/3) for unequal masses.

For three or more sources, relevance-filtered portions of these pairwise balance
surfaces assemble into a many-source parity network. The network is a derived
diagnostic geometry of gravitational readability. It is not a force boundary, a
physical barrier, or a modification of general relativity. Its purpose is to
make source-readable balance and transition structure explicit inside a declared
weak-field comparison rule.

This paper gives the minimal mathematical construction, benchmark cases, and
local existence conditions for many-source parity networks. Broader questions
about mesoscale interpretation and proof-of-concept extraction results are
reserved for companion work.

Keywords:
weak-field gravity, general relativity, tidal field, parity surface,
gravitational distinguishability, source readability, many-body systems,
diagnostic geometry

================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The gravitational coherence surface was introduced to describe where a
source-specific gravitational signal remains readable against its environment.
It is not a dynamical stability boundary. Matter can cross it freely. Orbits
need not respond sharply. It is a boundary of distinguishability: the location
where a chosen source-readable signal reaches parity with a calibrated
environmental threshold.

This paper asks the many-source version of that question.

If several sources are present, one can compare their source-readable
contributions directly. Where does source i become as readable as source j?
Where do those equality structures meet? How do they assemble when more than
two sources are present?

The construction is intentionally conservative. Work in a declared weak-field
regime. Assign each source a readable diagnostic contribution Q_i. Compare
sources pairwise through

    Psi_ij = Q_i - Q_j.

The parity surface for sources i and j is the zero set

    Psi_ij = 0.

In two-source settings, this surface is the balance surface between the two
source-readable contributions. In many-source settings, relevance-filtered
portions of such surfaces can assemble into a parity network.

This paper does not claim a new force, a new field equation, or a new physical
barrier. It gives the minimal mathematical scaffold for a derived readability
geometry.

================================================================================
2. SCOPE OF THIS PAPER
================================================================================

This paper has a narrow scope.

It defines:
- source-readable contributions Q_i,
- pairwise comparison fields Psi_ij,
- parity surfaces Sigma_ij,
- two-source benchmark behavior,
- three-source benchmark behavior,
- local smoothness conditions,
- and the many-source parity network as a relevance-filtered union of retained
  pairwise faces.

It does not report extraction results. It does not argue the full interpretive
importance of the mesoscale layer. It does not describe implementation details,
sampling strategies, or solver architecture.

The result should be read as a public mathematical theory paper. It supplies the
clean construction that later interpretive and results work can cite without
having to rederive the foundations.

================================================================================
3. WEAK-FIELD SETUP
================================================================================

Let a finite collection of gravitating sources S_i have masses M_i and positions
x_i on a chosen weak-field time slice. Let x be a point in the modeled domain,
and define

    r_i(x) = ||x - x_i||.

A softened distance may be used in numerical work,

    r_i,eps(x) = sqrt(||x - x_i||^2 + eps^2),

but the analytic benchmark cases below use the unsoftened expression away from
source locations.

The source-readable contribution Q_i is a nonnegative diagnostic scalar derived
from the weak-field contribution of source i. The narrow branch emphasized here
is the point-mass tidal scaling

    Q_i(x) proportional to M_i / r_i(x)^3.

This branch is motivated by the leading tidal falloff of a weak-field point
mass. It is not a replacement for the metric and is not an observable by itself.
It is a deliberately simple readable proxy used to make the first parity
construction analytically transparent.

A richer weak-field treatment may carry the tidal tensor

    E_ab approximately partial_a partial_b Phi

or source-attributed contributions E_ab^(i), and then define a scalar readable
quantity by projection or norm. Those richer branches are not required for the
minimal construction developed here.

================================================================================
4. PAIRWISE PARITY SURFACES
================================================================================

Given two source-readable contributions Q_i and Q_j, define the pairwise
comparison field

    Psi_ij(x) = Q_i(x) - Q_j(x).

The pairwise parity surface is

    Sigma_ij = { x : Psi_ij(x) = 0 }.

The sign of Psi_ij records which source has the larger readable contribution
under the declared diagnostic:

    Psi_ij > 0:
    source i is more readable than source j.

    Psi_ij < 0:
    source j is more readable than source i.

    Psi_ij = 0:
    the two contributions are in parity.

This is a statement about equality of a declared readable diagnostic. It is not
a statement that force vanishes or that motion changes discontinuously.

Under the point-mass tidal proxy,

    Q_i = k M_i / r_i^3,
    Q_j = k M_j / r_j^3,

where k is a common proportionality factor. Pairwise parity gives

    M_i / r_i^3 = M_j / r_j^3,

or equivalently,

    r_i / r_j = (M_i / M_j)^(1/3).

================================================================================
5. TWO-SOURCE BENCHMARKS
================================================================================

5.1 Equal masses

For M_i = M_j, the parity condition becomes

    r_i = r_j.

The parity surface is therefore the set of points equidistant from the two
sources. In Euclidean space this is the perpendicular bisector plane of the
segment joining the sources. In a two-dimensional cross-section it appears as
the midpoint line.

[Insert Figure 1: Equal-mass two-source parity]

This is the simplest analytic test of the construction.

5.2 Unequal masses

For unequal masses,

    r_i / r_j = (M_i / M_j)^(1/3).

If M_i > M_j, then parity requires r_i > r_j. The equality surface therefore
lies closer to the lower-mass source. The stronger source remains readable from
farther away, so the weaker source must be approached more closely for its
contribution to match.

[Insert Figure 2: Unequal-mass parity surface deformation]

This one-third-power displacement law is the cleanest benchmark for
mass-sensitive deformation of the parity surface.

================================================================================
6. THREE-SOURCE BENCHMARK
================================================================================

The equal-mass equilateral three-source case gives the simplest nontrivial
many-source benchmark.

Place three equal masses at the vertices of an equilateral triangle. Under the
point-mass tidal proxy,

    Q_i proportional to M / r_i^3.

For each pair,

    Q_i = Q_j  iff  r_i = r_j.

Thus each pairwise parity surface is the perpendicular bisector plane of the
corresponding source pair. In the plane of the sources, the three bisectors meet
at the circumcenter. In three dimensions, the corresponding pairwise surfaces
intersect along a line perpendicular to the triangle plane.

[Insert Figure 3: Equal-mass three-body parity benchmark]

This benchmark introduces triple-balance structure. A triple-balance locus
satisfies

    Q_i = Q_j = Q_k.

In symmetric scenes this structure is analytically simple. In perturbed scenes,
it provides a useful test of whether extracted geometry deforms continuously
under controlled changes of mass or source position.

================================================================================
7. LOCAL EXISTENCE AND SEAM CONDITIONS
================================================================================

The parity surfaces used here have ordinary local existence conditions.

Let Q_i and Q_j be differentiable in an open region U, and define

    Psi_ij = Q_i - Q_j.

If there exists x_* in U such that

    Psi_ij(x_*) = 0

and

    grad Psi_ij(x_*) != 0,

then the implicit function theorem implies that the local zero set
Psi_ij = 0 is a smooth embedded surface near x_*.

For three sources i, j, and k, a triple-balance set satisfies

    Q_i = Q_j = Q_k.

Equivalently, two independent residuals vanish, for example

    Psi_ij = 0,
    Psi_ik = 0.

When the corresponding parity surfaces intersect transversely in three
dimensions, their intersection is generically one-dimensional. In a
two-dimensional cross-section, it appears as a pointlike junction.

These statements are local. They do not assert that every equality surface is
globally meaningful or observationally relevant.

================================================================================
8. MANY-SOURCE PARITY NETWORKS
================================================================================

For a source set {S_i}, every pair defines a candidate comparison field

    Psi_ij = Q_i - Q_j.

The raw collection of zero sets Psi_ij = 0 may be large. In a many-source
system, however, not every algebraic equality surface is equally meaningful.
Two sources may have a formal equality in a region where neither source is
contextually relevant to the local readable structure.

The many-source parity network is therefore defined as a retained union of
relevance-filtered pairwise faces:

    N = union over retained pairs I_ij,

where I_ij is the retained portion of the pairwise parity surface between
sources i and j.

The filtering criterion depends on the declared diagnostic purpose. At minimum,
the retained face should participate in the local source-readable partition or
transition structure of the modeled scene. This prevents the network from being
identified with every algebraic zero crossing anywhere in the domain.

[Insert Figure 4: Relevance filtering in a many-source scene]

This distinction is essential. Pairwise residuals provide lawful comparison
channels. The parity network is a derived retained structure.

================================================================================
9. RELATION TO THE GRAVITATIONAL COHERENCE SURFACE
================================================================================

The single-source gravitational coherence surface compares a source-readable
signal with a calibrated background or environmental threshold:

    source signal = environmental threshold.

The many-source parity construction compares source-readable signals with one
another:

    source signal i = source signal j.

[Insert Figure 5: From gravitational coherence to source-source parity]

Both constructions are diagnostic comparisons. Both concern gravitational
readability. Neither introduces a force cutoff.

The many-source construction can be viewed as the source-source extension of the
coherence idea. Instead of asking where one source fades into the environment,
it asks where multiple source-readable contributions become mutually balanced.

================================================================================
10. LIMITS AND NON-CLAIMS
================================================================================

The present construction is intentionally limited.

It does not modify general relativity.

It does not introduce a new force.

It does not define a physical wall or membrane.

It does not by itself imply orbital stability or instability.

It does not claim that every zero crossing is meaningful.

It does not claim that the parity network is a fundamental invariant.

It does not replace tidal tensors, metrics, geodesic deviation, or numerical
relativity.

It does not claim observational detection.

The claim is narrower: once a source-readable weak-field diagnostic is declared,
pairwise equality structures and their retained many-source network can be
defined, benchmarked, and studied as diagnostic geometry.

================================================================================
11. OUTLOOK
================================================================================

The next theoretical steps are straightforward.

First, benchmark the equal-mass and unequal-mass two-source cases.

Second, benchmark the equal-mass equilateral three-source case and controlled
perturbations.

Third, study which portions of raw pairwise equality surfaces should be retained
as meaningful faces in many-source scenes.

Fourth, compare the simple scalar readable branch Q_i proportional to M_i/r_i^3
against richer weak-field tidal tensor diagnostics.

Fifth, distinguish mathematical parity structure from operationally useful
parity structure through stability and sensitivity testing.

The broader interpretation of these structures as a mesoscale descriptive layer
belongs to companion work. Proof-of-concept extraction results belong to a
separate results paper. The present paper remains focused on the core
mathematical construction.

================================================================================
12. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The gravitational coherence surface provided a way to ask where a source remains
gravitationally readable against its environment. The many-source parity network
extends that readability logic to source-source comparison.

The construction is compact:

    declare Q_i,
    form Psi_ij = Q_i - Q_j,
    extract the parity surface Psi_ij = 0,
    retain the portions that participate in the meaningful many-source
    readability network.

In the point-mass tidal proxy Q_i proportional to M_i/r_i^3, the benchmark
geometry is analytically transparent. Equal masses produce midpoint surfaces.
Unequal masses obey the one-third-power distance law. Three equal sources
produce triple-balance structure.

This is not new gravity. It is a mathematical scaffold for derived
gravitational readability geometry in weak-field multi-source systems.
