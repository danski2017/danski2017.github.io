Title:
The Gravitational Coherence Surface and Shared Parity Network Framework:
A Canonical Postulate with Interpretive Extension

======================================================================
LAYER 1 — CANONICAL FORMULATION
For paper-facing, tracker-facing, and solver-facing use
======================================================================

Core Postulate

Within standard general relativity in the weak-field regime, a gravitating
system may be equipped with a derived relational geometry defined by parity
among source-readable gravitational signals.

In single-source or isotropic competing-floor settings, this geometry reduces
to the shell-like Gravitational Coherence Surface (GCS): the locus where a
chosen source-readable signal reaches parity with a calibrated competing floor.

In genuinely many-source settings, the same parity logic induces a shared
parity network: a piecewise-smooth partition geometry composed of
relevance-filtered balanced interfaces and junction structure on a chosen
time slice.

This framework does not modify Einstein’s equations, does not introduce a new
force law, does not assert a physical barrier to motion, and does not identify
distinguishability boundaries with dynamical stability boundaries. Its purpose
is narrower and more disciplined: to define a geometric bookkeeping layer for
where source-readable gravitational structure transitions between source-
dominant and shared sectors.

Axiom 1 — Source-Readable Comparison Language

The framework begins by choosing a source-readable gravitational signal Q_i for
each source S_i on a spacelike time slice Sigma_t.

The comparison language must be explicit. Depending on regime, Q_i may be
constructed from a monopole readability proxy, a directional tidal amplitude,
a projection of the source-attributed electric Weyl tensor, or another stated
weak-field source-resolved diagnostic.

The framework does not require that all legitimate signal choices produce the
same geometry. It requires only that the comparison language be fixed and used
consistently.

Axiom 2 — Parity Construction

Given source-readable fields Q_i, parity is defined by equality against a
declared competitor.

In the single-source shell setting, parity is reached where a source-readable
signal equals a prescribed competing floor.

In the many-source setting, the most important local case is pairwise parity,
defined by

    Q_i = Q_j.

These equalities generate balanced sets that separate source-dominant sectors.

Axiom 3 — From Shell to Network

The shell is the isotropic special case of a broader parity construction.

If parity is reached at the same distance in every direction around a source,
the result is a shell.

If parity is directional, anisotropic, or shaped by the competition of
multiple comparable sources, the result is no longer a single enclosing shell
but a network of balanced interfaces.

The logical progression is

    shell -> directional parity points -> parity cloud -> balanced sheet
    -> relevance-filtered face -> junction structure
    -> shared parity network.

Axiom 4 — Relevance Filtering

Not every algebraic equality belongs to the physically meaningful geometric
partition.

The retained network is formed from exposed, relevance-filtered interfaces:
those balanced sets that actually separate neighboring source-dominant cells.

This distinction prevents overcounting latent equalities that are buried under
stronger competitors and preserves the partition geometry as the meaningful
many-body object.

Axiom 5 — Junction Structure

Where several exposed interfaces meet, the network develops junction sets.

In a three-dimensional time slice, generic pairwise balanced faces meet along
one-dimensional seams. In symmetric three-body cases this produces the first
exact foam seed: three faces meeting along a shared junction line.

Higher-order junction structure is a natural part of the network and reflects
irreducibly shared balance among multiple sources.

Axiom 6 — Weak-Field First Explicit Regime

The first explicit formal regime for Atlas uses weak-field source-readable
tidal structure.

For point-mass tidal proxies,

    Q_i ~ M_i / r_i^3,

and pairwise balance gives

    M_i / r_i^3 = M_j / r_j^3.

For equal masses, the balanced interface reduces to the geometric midpoint
sheet. For unequal masses, the interface bends toward the lower-mass source.
This supplies the first concrete extraction law for the three-body benchmark
program.

Axiom 7 — Center-Triad Diagnostics

The framework distinguishes three important centers, not as foundational
axioms of gravity, but as diagnostics of the extracted geometry:

1. geometric centroid
2. mass barycenter
3. parity node

The parity node is the location minimizing source-readability disparity under
the chosen rule. Offsets among these three centers provide a compact diagnostic
language for asymmetry, deformation, and geometric handoff in many-body
configurations.

These centers are diagnostic outputs of the framework, not yet canonical
invariants.

Axiom 8 — Geodesic Relation

The shared parity network may serve as a useful relational datum for geodesic
analysis, but geodesic anchoring is secondary to the present formalism.

The current framework does not claim that geodesics physically snap to the
network or that interface crossings impose a dynamical discontinuity. It claims
only that once the network is defined, geodesics may be studied relative to
that partition geometry.

Axiom 9 — Interpretation Discipline

The GCS and shared parity network are best understood as derived geometric
objects for tracking gravitational readability and balance across scale.

They are not new gravity.
They are not horizons.
They are not force cutoffs.
They are not yet observationally established.
They are not substitutes for the full dynamical content of the N-body problem.

They are a structured language for organizing how source-attributed
gravitational geometry is partitioned, exposed, and compared.

Canonical Implication

Under this formulation, Atlas Solver is not a theory premise but an instrument
for extracting, visualizing, measuring, and archiving the resulting parity
geometry in reproducible weak-field many-body benchmarks.

The first practical target remains narrow and clear:
stable extraction of the shared parity network in weak-field three-body
systems, together with center-triad diagnostics and archive-ready outputs.

LAYER 2 — INTERPRETIVE EXTENSION
For speculative framing, philosophy, and future hypothesis-building
Not canonical solver language
======================================================================

Interpretive Thesis

The formal parity framework invites a broader interpretive picture.

A many-body gravitational system may be imagined as organizing space into
temporary regions of local coherence, where source-readable structure remains
legible against what surrounds it. These regions behave, in descriptive terms,
like nested and deformable coherence domains.

The shell is the nursery form of such a domain.
The network is its mature many-body expression.

Within this picture, space is not interpreted as passively holding a single
flat stage beneath all sources, but as carrying layered competing readability
fields. The geometry of a system is then not exhausted by summing influences
everywhere at once. It also includes the loci where source-readability passes
from one local organization to another.

On this interpretive reading, the shared parity network can be spoken of as a
geometry of influence handoff.

Speculative Vocabulary

Terms such as sovereign bubble, custody handoff, topological erasure, and
recursive boundary may be used as descriptive metaphors for this process, but
they should be treated as interpretive language rather than established formal
claims.

Under that metaphor:

- a local mass group may be pictured as sustaining a temporary coherence domain,
- the boundary of that domain is where its readable structure reaches parity
  with competitors,
- the loss of distinguishability can be pictured as a handover of geometric
  salience to a higher-order environment,
- and the many-body network becomes a foam-like map of shared gravitational
  jurisdiction.

This vocabulary may be useful for intuition, but it is not part of the narrow
formal claim.

Speculative Dynamic Picture

One may also speculate that moving bodies experience many-body geometry not
merely as a bulk superposition problem, but as motion through successive
regions of source-dominant and shared curvature readability.

In that picture, the parity network could become a useful descriptor of where
gravitational influence is handed off, negotiated, or blended.

But this remains interpretive unless and until geodesic statistics, solver
benchmarks, and parameter sweeps show robust correlations between trajectory
behavior and extracted parity geometry.

Speculative Cosmological Reach

At larger scales, one might wonder whether nested parity structures and their
deformations contribute a useful language for describing morphology-sensitive
gravitational organization that is blurred by conventional averaging.

This is a legitimate future hypothesis direction.

It is not, at present, a justified replacement for dark matter, a claim of new
effective stress-energy, or an established explanation of galaxy-scale
anomalies.

Any such extension must be earned through:
- stability of extracted structures,
- reproducibility across sweeps,
- geodesic correlation,
- controlled comparison against standard models,
- and careful separation of diagnostic geometry from physical observables.

Interpretive Summary

The canonical theory says:
    parity defines a derived geometry.

The speculative extension says:
    that derived geometry may be a useful language for how spacetime organizes
    readable gravitational structure across nested many-body systems.

That stronger reading is not yet proven.
But it may become the philosophical horizon toward which the disciplined solver
program points.
