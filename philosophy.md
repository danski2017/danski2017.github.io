<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">A Proton Walks Into a Void</h2>

Imagine a single proton floating in the emptiness... where does the proton end? 

The Atlas program has been asking a different question. Not where the charge ends. Where the *gravitational identity* ends.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">Jurisdiction, Not Force</h2>

Gravity has always been described as a force... but there's a layer of gravitational structure that this description doesn't naturally capture. Not the force itself — the *territory* the force organizes.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.3em; color: #222; margin-top: 25px;">The Parity Condition</h3>

The boundary of a jurisdiction is a geometric equality: 

$$\Phi_{1}(\vec{r}) = \Phi_{2}(\vec{r})$$

In the Atlas framework, this is the "zero-margin" line where identity is contested.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">The Soap Bubble Inside the Proton</h2>

When Atlas applies this framework to the three quarks inside a proton, something beautiful happens... three soap bubbles, fighting for space. The heavier one wins more than its fair share.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">The Boundary We Declare</h2>

The sphere exists because context exists. Remove the context and the sphere dissolves. The proton's outer boundary isn't intrinsic. It's conferred by everything around it.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">You Can't Know the Parents</h2>

You're always in the middle. Always holding one slice of an infinite nesting. Always with fog in both directions.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">One Language, Every Scale</h2>

The universe is not a collection of separate physics at separate scales. It's one nested structure... telling the truth.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">The Observer as Architect</h2>

If the proton's boundary is conferred by its context, what does that say about the human observer? In this article, we explore how the "Scene Declaration" in the Atlas Solver mirrors the cognitive act of **Relevance Realization**. 

Just as the solver must choose which parent fields to include, the mind must prune an infinite field of data to create a coherent jurisdiction of thought.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.6em; color: #111; margin-top: 30px;">The Confidence Record of the Soul</h2>

When we approximate the "Parent Sources"—the things we cannot resolve but know are there—we are essentially mapping the boundaries of our own faith in the structure of the world.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.3em; color: #222; margin-top: 25px;">Recursive Identity</h3>

If identity is contested at the parity surface, then our "self" is not a point, but a boundary condition. We exist exactly where our internal pressures match the external context.
