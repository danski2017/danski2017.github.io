A preliminary Atlas Solver lab note on scalar rotation, electric-Weyl structure, eigenframe orientation, relational witnesses, and coarse-graining transfer functions.

Date: 2026-05-13
Status: internal public-facing draft spine, not a publication claim.

## Overview Atlas has begun a controlled study of mesoscale curvature loss in galaxy-scale coarse-graining. In toy Milky-Way-like simulations, common averaging methods can retain recognizable scalar rotation behavior while substantially altering the local electric-Weyl tensor and eigenframe structure seen by relational witnesses. The current evidence trail spans source-count ladders, coarse-resolution ladders, morphology variants, witness-field variants, and a first cosmic-web averaging probe. The result is not a claim about dark matter or new physics. It is a methods result about what information survives averaging.
Core claim shape

Standard coarse-graining choices can preserve useful scalar rotation information while materially altering the local electric-Weyl structure and eigenframe orientation seen by relational witnesses.

Claim status

This is a controlled weak-field curvature proxy study inside a GR-compatible language of curvature, witnesses, and averaging. It is not a dark-matter claim, not a challenge to GR or SR, not full-GR numerical relativity, and not an observational detection.

## The Problem Galaxy models are often reduced to scalar summaries. Rings, sectors, grids, kernel maps, tilted-ring reductions, and low-order mass proxies are useful and often necessary. They are also filters. Atlas asks what happens to local curvature structure during that reduction. The lab's interest is mesoscale curvature: the structure between individual sources and global summary observables. ## The Method The protocol builds retained many-source scenes and compares them against coarse-grained branches using the same relational witness panels. It tracks scalar rotation, electric-Weyl Frobenius structure, eigenvalue span, and eigenframe alignment. The scalar side tracks rotation-level behavior, such as circular-speed-like or rotation-curve-style summaries. The tensor side tracks local tidal-curvature organization through electric-Weyl structure and eigenframe orientation. Relational witnesses are the declared sampling sites from which those local structures are read. The result does not say that scalar summaries are wrong. It says scalar agreement alone does not certify preservation of local curvature-orientation structure. ## Simulation Sequence

B006: 500-source control

A Milky-Way-like rotating disc with retained and coarse branches. The run reproduced the initial pattern: electric-Weyl and eigenframe deltas were several times larger than scalar rotation deltas.

B007: 1000-source coarse-method matrix

Added tilted rings, annular models, sector annular models, particle mesh, Gaussian/kernel smoothing, and low-order multipole proxies. The result showed that the delta is method-dependent, not a single artifact of one averaging recipe.

B008: 2000-source observational benchmark

Added inclined line-of-sight projection, beam smoothing, surface-density maps, and tilted-ring reconstruction. The observational branch complicated the story in a useful way: some reductions damage scalar rotation too, while surface-density, grid, and kernel branches preserve the tensor/scalar distinction more clearly.

B009: source-count scale ladder

Ran 2000, 5000, and 10000 source-proxy scenes with fixed witnesses. The right scaling read appears convergence-like rather than a simple multiplication by source count:

Delta(N) = Delta_inf + A / sqrt(N)

Increasing source count suppresses finite-source noise and exposes a method-dependent residual floor.

B010: coarse-method resolution ladder

Held source count fixed at 10000 while refining rings, sectors, grids, and kernels. Scalar rotation disagreement generally decreased with resolution, but electric-Weyl and eigenframe disagreement did not collapse in lockstep. This is one of the clearest signs that the tensor-side story is not simply the same observable under a different label.

B011: morphology variants

Repeated the protocol across barred, bulge-heavy, thick-disk, flocculent, and disturbed outer-disk variants. The tensor/scalar split persisted across morphology, supporting a method-level story rather than a one-scene accident.

B012: witness-field robustness

Repeated the protocol across midplane, off-plane, halo, radial-spoke, and survey-wedge witness panels. The strongest signal lives in the disc-adjacent, off-plane, spoke, and survey-wedge panels. The halo-style panel is quieter for some methods, which helps locate the effect rather than overstate it.

B013: cosmic-web averaging probe

Built a weak-field clustered, filament, wall, and void proxy scene and compared the retained source field against cartesian grids, Gaussian density smoothing, spherical averaging, and component-centroid compression. The result extends the evidence trail from galaxy coarse-graining toward the averaging and backreaction controversy: geometric witness data and scalar acceleration summaries do not survive averaging as a single universal transfer function.

## The Pattern Across B006-B012, scalar rotation and local curvature structure do not move together. Refining source count and method resolution sharpens rather than erases the distinction in several branches. B013 extends the same lab question into a cosmic-web proxy scene: averaging has a method-dependent geometric transfer function. ## Key Numbers Ratios above 1 mean the tensor-side or eigenframe disagreement is larger than the scalar disagreement under the same comparison. The cleanest reading is that different diagnostic layers survive coarse-graining differently. Scalar rotation summaries can remain useful while the local electric-Weyl/eigenframe structure read by relational witnesses changes more strongly. That separates scalar preservation from curvature-orientation preservation. The simulations suggest a measurable transfer-function problem in common coarse-graining operations. Atlas is trying to make that transfer function explicit rather than treating averaging as a neutral passage from detailed scene to summary observable. ## What This Does Not Claim - It does not explain galaxy rotation curves. - It is not evidence for or against dark matter. - It does not prove standard averaging is wrong. - It is not full-GR numerical relativity. - It does not claim toy galaxies reproduce real galaxies. - It does not claim scalar rotation summaries are useless. ## Next Run The next step is a publication-grade convergence suite with higher source-proxy counts, declared coarse-resolution parameters, multiple witness panels, locked metrics, and observationally anchored scene design. If the tensor/scalar split survives those tests, it becomes a serious methods result: a sign that galaxy-scale and cosmic-web summaries may preserve useful scalar behavior while altering the mesoscale curvature structure that relational witnesses read.
